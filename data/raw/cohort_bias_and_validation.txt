Title: Cohort Bias and Validation of Biomedical AI Claims

Biomedical AI-generated insights should be evaluated against the structure and quality of the cohort used to produce them.

Common cohort-level concerns:
- sample size too small
- excessive missingness in key clinical variables
- subgroup imbalance by sex, age, ancestry, disease stage, or treatment exposure
- confounding between disease severity and treatment assignment
- single-center bias or measurement bias

Validation implication:
An AI-generated insight may sound plausible but still be unreliable if the supporting cohort is too small or imbalanced. A validation engine should check whether the cohort has enough samples, acceptable missingness, and reasonable subgroup representation.

Decision layer behavior:
- Strong evidence plus consistent cohort structure may support a validated output.
- Plausible but underpowered evidence may support a weakly_supported output.
- Contradictory evidence or implausible directionality may support a rejected output.

This document is intended for local testing of clinical AI validation workflows.
